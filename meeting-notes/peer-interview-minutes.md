## 1. Purpose
To present the assessment improvement project to a peer and get feedback on the overall project and on John's area of responsibility, the Professional Development Rewards System (PDRS).

## 2. Presentation of the project (John Byrne)

### 2.1 Problem
- Students were not getting timely feedback on their assessments.
- When feedback was given, it was often only "Not Yet Satisfactory" (NYS), with no guidance on what to do next. Other assessors gave very detailed feedback but took too long.
- Because practices varied across assessors, results were not updated in the LMS. The admin team could not trust the LMS or tell where a student was up to when students called.

### 2.2 Proposed initiatives
1. **Joint taskforce:** assessors and the admin team working together on continuous quality improvement, to ease tension between the two teams.
2. **Assessor toolkit:** built by the taskforce. Includes a student feedback template, effectively a standard operating procedure for assessors. It must work for workplace, remote, face-to-face and online assessors.
3. **Organisational Awards Night:** probably biannual. Public recognition of excellence and improvement, through staff nominations and management picks. Winners get recognition they can add to their CV.
4. **PDRS (John's responsibility):** "Hit your KPI, get extra PD."
   - Triggered when the monthly KPI is met.
   - PD is the recipient's choice and does not have to come from a set list. It is over and above standard PD.
   - Within the price range: go ahead. Over the allowance: bank it and combine it with the next month's reward, or pay the difference.
   - KPIs encourage the new practices: prompt LMS updates, **10-day-or-less turnaround**, and helpful feedback.

## 3. Peer feedback (Tina Foster)

### 3.1 Overall project
- **Supports the taskforce** and a toolkit that works for everyone.
- **Supports a standard operating procedure for feedback.** Feedback should have clear parameters: on each NYS, what the student did well, what they did not, and what to improve. John confirmed this is the intent, with all assessors following the same content.
- **Less convinced about the rewards and the awards night.** She felt they don't link closely to the problem, and questioned rewarding people for doing the job they are paid to do.
  - *John's response:* recognition and encouragement work better than penalties. Meeting KPIs is part of the job, but the human factor still matters.
- **Suggested practical process changes** to support assessors, such as:
  - another assessor stepping in when someone is absent
  - staff with the right skills marking basic or straightforward questions
  - other practical changes for the assessor and admin teams
  - *John's response:* much of this sits within the taskforce's scope.
- **Asked whether too much has been "lumped into" the taskforce** when some items could be separate projects or improvements.
  - *John's response:* the taskforce is given the problems because the people who experience them know the job best, and the two teams need to solve it together. For example, one online assessor already achieves a 48-hour turnaround using scripts and automations, but hasn't shared these. The taskforce gives a way to share practices like this. John will take the point back to the group.
- **Suggested fleshing out practical procedures as defined tasks.** John agreed the taskforce could be seeded with defined project work, such as what the toolkit must contain and achieve, rather than a general brief to "fix everything".

### 3.2 Obstacles
- **Budget:** can the organisation fund the PDRS?
  - *John's response:* there are three costs. Taskforce time (perhaps one meeting a month), the awards night (sponsorship could offset this), and the PDRS. If the problem isn't fixed, the organisation will lose students and that revenue anyway.
- **Time:** taking trainers and assessors away from classes and students for the taskforce will be hard.
- **Admin team eligibility:** does the admin team have KPIs, and can they access the PDRS? Leaving them out would be seen as unfair.
  - *John's response:* this had not been considered and would be a problem if left out. The admin team should be included.
- **Fairness and competitiveness:** a consistent high achiever could dominate and single others out. She suggested including participation awards at the awards night.
  - *John's clarification:* the PDRS is a **collective team KPI**. When a team (e.g. assessors, admin) meets its goal, the **team** receives the PD and chooses it.
- **Rest of the organisation:** Tina asked about staff outside the assessor and admin teams.
  - *John's response:* it is a small RTO. The awards night is organisation-wide, and the PDRS could become a company-wide policy for any team that meets its KPIs.

### 3.3 Missed opportunities
- Look more closely at the **systems and processes** within the assessor and admin teams, and embed the improvements in those processes, rather than relying on KPIs, awards and PD.
- **Risk raised by John:** the people creating the problem are being asked to solve it. The taskforce will need clear direction and specific project work so it tackles the problem from the right angles.
- **Independent lead:** Tina suggested an independent project lead to keep the taskforce on track and neutral. This could be an external consultant, or one or two people from another team such as a CQI team, so the leadership isn't seen as coming from either the assessors or the admin team.

### 3.4 The assessor's perspective
- From an assessor's view, the taskforce and a shared toolkit are a **great opportunity**.
- She still felt it is **unfair if the admin team doesn't get the PDRS**. John agreed there is no reason they can't.
